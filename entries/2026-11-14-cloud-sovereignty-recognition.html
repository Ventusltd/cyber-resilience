Cloud sovereignty levels and the case for early UK recognition

The article

Britain should seek recognition under Europe's cloud sovereignty levels before the rules settle — Kate Bennett, Compare the Cloud, 8 November 2026. Opinion article.

The author argues that the UK should ask now to be treated as an associated third country under the EU's proposed Cloud and AI Development Act, so that UK-controlled cloud providers could be assessed for the higher assurance levels of the EU's sovereignty framework while the text is still being drafted. The piece sets out objections as well, including how far EU scrutiny of UK law would reach and how much recognition would deliver in practice.

Why it is logged here

This log records single-provider dependency in critical systems. Sovereignty tiers are one of the ways governments are responding to that dependency: they decide which providers may host which workloads, and so where data centres, and the grid capacity behind them, are built. The 13 November entry describes the outages and concentration that prompt such rules.

This entry is a pointer to an external opinion piece, not an endorsement of its conclusions. The proposed EU Act and any UK position were not final at the date of this entry; read the article and the primary texts it cites.
